For a long time my personal site survived on technicality. It existed, so I could tell myself I had one.

That stopped being good enough a while ago.

The old version was a single-page Bootstrap site from a different stage of my career. It was not embarrassing exactly, but it was clearly old, thin, and a bit too tied to who I used to be professionally. It said almost nothing about the kinds of products I work on now or the standard of work I want associated with my name.

The visual age was the obvious problem. The structural problem was worse. A single static page gives you nowhere sensible to put project detail, writing, or anything with a bit of nuance. Everything gets flattened into a résumé-shaped lump.

This rebuild is my attempt to fix that properly rather than keep patching around it.

## What I want the site to do

I do not need the site to do much, but I do need it to do a few things well:

- explain what I actually do now
- show projects with enough context to be worth reading
- make writing part of the site rather than a neglected extra
- stay easy to maintain without dragging in a CMS or a lot of ceremony

That last bit matters more than it sounds. If publishing a project page or a short post feels annoying, I will put it off, and the site will quietly rot again.

## Why Astro was the right fit

I wanted something modern, but I did not want to build a tiny media business just to maintain a personal site.

Astro fits the shape of the job nicely. It is content first, fast by default, and happy to stay mostly static. I can add structure without committing to a stack that demands constant attention.

The other useful choice is keeping the content in git. That gives the site a natural place to live alongside everything else I build, and it removes most of the friction that usually kills personal sites over time.

## What still needs work

The structure is now in place, but the quality still comes down to details.

The obvious gaps are better project screenshots, stronger case-study pages, and a profile photo that looks less like it was rescued from an older internet. That is the last stretch now. The important thing is that the site finally has enough shape to justify finishing.
