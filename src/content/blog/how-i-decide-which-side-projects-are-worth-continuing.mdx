Most side projects do not need more effort. They need a cleaner kill test.

I have become less impressed by how exciting an idea feels in week one. Almost anything can feel promising while it is still running on novelty. The better question is whether the thing keeps pulling once the easy excitement is gone.

## The questions I keep coming back to

Before I keep investing in a side project, I usually ask a few blunt questions:

- do I actually care about the problem, or just the idea of the problem?
- has the thing become useful in real life, not just in theory?
- does the product get clearer as I work on it, or murkier?
- am I learning something worth carrying into the next project?

If the answer is mostly no, the honest move is to stop.

## Momentum is not the same as product pull

This is the bit I have had to learn repeatedly.

A project can have momentum because I already wrote code, made a nice screen, or told people about it. None of that means it deserves a future. Sometimes the cleanest decision is to admit the idea was interesting, keep the lesson, and move on.

That sounds a bit harsh, but it is usually healthier than dragging a half-alive side project around for another six months.
