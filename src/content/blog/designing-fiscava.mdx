Fiscava became more interesting the moment I stopped treating it like an expense tracker.

Recording transactions is table stakes. Plenty of products can do that. The harder problem, and the one I actually care about, is helping someone understand what their money is doing and what they should do next.

That sounds obvious, but it changes the shape of the product quite a bit.

## Expense trackers hit a ceiling quickly

Simple trackers are useful for capture. After that, many of them flatten out.

Once someone wants better context, clearer summaries, or a stronger sense of progress, the product has to grow beyond categories and charts. The job is no longer just logging activity. It is helping form a usable picture.

## More features is usually the lazy answer

The easiest move is to add more charts, more categories, more breakdowns, more settings.

Most of the time that is just complexity dressed up as progress. The better move is to ask what decision the product is actually trying to support, then shape the model around that.

That has been the more useful direction for Fiscava. Not bigger for the sake of it. Broader where the product genuinely needs a better view of the person's financial position.

## The interesting part is product judgement

Finance software can get bloated very quickly because almost every idea sounds defensible in isolation.

The hard part is deciding what deserves to be first class, what can stay implied, and what should be rejected before it turns the whole product into homework.
